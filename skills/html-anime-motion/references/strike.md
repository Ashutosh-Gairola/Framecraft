# Strikes — punch, kick, block, hit reaction

Bodies hitting bodies. Contact on ones; acting on twos.

## Punch, 5 keys

1. Guard: fists at the chin, elbows in, feet a shoulder apart, lead foot forward.
2. Anticipation: the hips and shoulders turn away, the striking fist drops a little. 4f.
3. Extension: hips turn first, then shoulder, then the arm snaps straight. The fist turns palm-down at the end. The rear heel lifts. A short straight smear of the arm. 1–2f on ones.
4. Contact: the knuckles of the first two fingers on the target, arm not fully locked. Hold 2f. Impact lines or a flash.
5. Recoil: the fist returns along the same line, faster than it went out, back to guard.

A hook travels on a horizontal arc around the body's axis; an uppercut on a vertical arc from the hip.

## Kick, 5 keys

1. Guard. 2. Chamber: knee up, foot tucked, the standing leg bends. 4f. 3. Extension: the leg snaps out from the knee; the standing foot pivots, the body leans away to balance. 4. Contact with the ball of the foot (front kick), the heel (side kick), or the shin (roundhouse). Hold 2f. 5. Re-chamber, then the foot returns to the floor. The arms counterbalance throughout.

## Block

The forearm meets the strike at an angle and deflects it, not stops it. The blocker's body moves back a little at contact. 2f hold at contact.

## Hit reaction, 4 keys

1. The impact drawing: the hit part moves first and furthest (the head snaps away from the fist). 2. The body follows: spine curves, feet slide or leave the ground. 3. The peak: the furthest point of the recoil, hair and cloth still traveling forward. 4. Settle or fall. The bigger the hit, the longer between 1 and 3 and the longer the hold at the end.

## Clash of two bodies

Both strikes meet at one point between them. Freeze on contact 2–3f with an impact flash, then both are pushed back along their attack lines. Who wins is shown by who recovers first.

## Common mistakes

- The arm moving without the hips. Power starts at the floor.
- Both fighters symmetrical.
- No contact frame: the fist passes through the target in one tween.
