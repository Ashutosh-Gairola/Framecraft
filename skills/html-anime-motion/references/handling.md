# Handling — hold, reach, catch, throw, everyday tools

Hands with things in them. Draw the hand and the object as one unit when held; the object gets its own group and pivot when it moves.

## The hand

- Four fingers and a thumb, counted the same in every drawing of the shot.
- A closed grip wraps the object: the fingers go around and the thumb closes against them. No gap between palm and object.
- A light grip (a cup, a letter) uses fingertips; a heavy or important one (a blade, a rope) uses the whole palm, and the wrist bends under the weight.
- The object's weight shows in the arm: heavy objects pull the shoulder down and the elbow straight.

## Reach and take, 4 keys

1. Eyes go to the object first. 2. The hand moves on an arc, wrist leading, fingers relaxed and slightly open. 3. Fingers open just before contact. 4. Close around it; a 2f hold on the contact; then the arm brings it back toward the body.

## Catch, 5 keys

1. Eyes track the object (it falls on its own arc, accelerating). 2. Hands rise to meet its path, palms toward it, fingers spread. 3. Contact: hold 2f. 4. Give: the hands travel with the object a short distance in its direction of travel, the arms bend to absorb it. A heavy object pulls the body down a step. 5. Settle, the object held, a beat of stillness. A caught light (a glowing thing) also lights the hands and face from below on contact.

## Throw, 5 keys

1. Wind up: arm back, weight on the back foot, the opposite arm points at the target. 2. Anticipation hold, 2–4f. 3. The release arc: hips, shoulder, elbow, wrist in that order, a smear of the forearm. 4. Release at the front of the arc; the object leaves on the tangent of that arc. 5. Follow-through: the arm crosses the body, the back foot steps forward.

## Everyday tools

| Prop | Grip | Pivot | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Umbrella | Fist around the handle, at shoulder height or just above | The hand | The canopy sits over the head and slightly behind; rain hits the canopy and runs off the rim edge. Wind tilts the canopy, not the handle alone |
| Cup / tea bowl | Fingertips around, thumb on the rim side; a bowl in two palms | The hand | Lifted with the elbow, not the wrist. The liquid stays level when the cup tilts |
| Phone | Fingers behind, thumb on the screen | The wrist | The screen is a `practical` light on the face from below |
| Lantern | Hanging from the hand by its cord or pole | The cord's top | It swings as a pendulum and lags every move of the hand by 1–2 drawings |
| Letter / paper | Fingertips at the edges, two hands to read | The fingers | Paper bends; it is never a rigid rectangle while held at one corner |
| Book | One hand under the spine, the other on the open page | The spine | Pages turn on an arc from the spine |
| Bag / satchel | Strap over the shoulder, hand on the strap | The shoulder | The bag swings opposite to the walk and bounces on the down step |
| Hammer / tool | Fist near the end of the handle | The wrist | Wind up slow, strike fast, 2f hold on contact |
| Flower | Fingertips on the stem | The fingers | Petals react to every move, lagging 1–2 drawings |

## Common mistakes

- The object floating near the hand without touching it.
- The object changing size between shots.
- A swinging object with no lag.
- Picking up a heavy thing with no change in the body.
