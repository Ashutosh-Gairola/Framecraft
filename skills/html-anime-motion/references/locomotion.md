# Locomotion — walk, run, jump, land, turn, dodge

On twos. A cycle is drawn once and repeated with `tl.set` swaps at frame boundaries; the background or the figure's position moves at the cycle's exact speed, or the feet slide.

## Walk, a 4-drawing cycle on twos (8 frames a step, 16 a stride)

1. **Contact**: front heel down, back toe down, legs at their widest, arms opposite to legs. The lowest-but-one head height.
2. **Down**: front knee bends to take the weight. The lowest point of the head.
3. **Passing**: the back leg swings past the standing leg, which is straight. The head's highest point.
4. **Up**: pushing off the back toe, the body tips forward toward the next contact.

Then the same four with legs and arms swapped. The head bobs about 1/12 of a head height. A slow, sad walk is 12 frames a step; a brisk one 6.

Position: the stride length divided by its frames gives the speed. In a pan, the background slides at that speed; the figure stays.

## Run, a 4-drawing cycle on twos (about 6 frames a step)

1. **Contact**: one foot lands under the body, not ahead of it. Knee bent.
2. **Down**: the deepest bend, body lowest.
3. **Push**: the back leg extends fully, the body launches forward.
4. **Float**: both feet off the ground, the highest point, front knee high.

The body leans forward; arms pump bent at 90°. A sprint goes to 4 frames a step and the lean increases.

## Jump, 6 keys

1. Stand. 2. **Anticipation**: crouch deep, arms swing back. 4–6f. 3. **Takeoff**: legs extend, arms throw up, a stretched drawing. 4. **Rise**: the body tucks; spacing gets smaller each frame (decelerating). 5. **Apex**: hold 2–4f. 6. **Fall**: spacing grows each frame (1, 3, 5, 7…), body opens to prepare the landing.

## Land, 3 keys

1. Contact on the balls of the feet, knees starting to bend. 2. **Squash**: the deepest crouch, hands may touch the ground, hair and cloth still falling. 3. Recover to standing over 6–8f, cloth and hair settling 2 drawings behind. In chibi style, squash the whole body 15% shorter on key 2.

## Turn (head and body)

Eyes lead, then the head, then the shoulders, then the hips and feet, each a drawing apart. A head turn includes a slight dip in the middle (the head lowers as it passes the front view). Blink on the turn: it hides the in-between and reads as natural.

## Dodge

Anticipation is small or none (it's a reaction). The body moves away on an arc, the head furthest, the feet last. Hold the extreme 2–4f so the miss reads, then recover.

## Common mistakes

- Sliding feet: the planted foot must stay fixed relative to the ground for its whole contact.
- Arms and legs on the same side moving together.
- A bounce tween standing in for a walk.
- Even spacing on a jump. Gravity needs growing or shrinking gaps.
