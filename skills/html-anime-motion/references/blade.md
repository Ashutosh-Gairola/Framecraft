# Blades — draw, cut, thrust, parry, clash, sheathe

Swords, katana, knives, spears, naginata. On ones while the blade moves fast; acting around it on twos.

## Grip and construction

- **Katana / two-handed sword**: right hand just under the guard, left hand at the pommel end, a fist's gap between them. Both wrists turned in, knuckles toward the edge.
- **One-handed blade / knife**: index finger knuckle against the guard; a knife in reverse grip has the blade along the forearm, edge out.
- **Spear**: back hand at the butt third, front hand a forearm's length ahead; the back hand drives, the front hand steers.
- Draw the blade as its own `<g>` with the pivot at the grip center. The hand is drawn over the handle, fingers closed around it, no gap.
- The edge faces the direction of the cut. The back of the blade (spine) is the thicker line.
- Blade length stays constant. Foreshortening shortens the drawn length only when the tip points at the camera; then widen the guard instead.

## Draw (unsheathe), 5 keys

1. Hand on the grip, scabbard at the hip, thumb pushing the guard (the koiguchi). Hold.
2. Anticipation: shoulders turn away, the elbow lifts. 4f.
3. The blade leaves: the scabbard is pulled back by the left hand as the right pulls forward. Smear of the blade along the scabbard line, 1–2f on ones.
4. Extreme: arm extended, blade horizontal or raised, past the target line.
5. Settle into guard (point at the opponent's eyes, both hands on the grip). Hold 12f+.

## Cut, 6 keys (the sakuga staple)

1. Ready / guard. Hold.
2. Anticipation: blade back over the opposite shoulder, weight on the back foot, hips coiled. 4f.
3. Smear: one stretched shape from the windup position to past contact, the blade arc as a tapered crescent in `practical` or `line`. No hand detail on this frame. 1f on ones.
4. Contact: the edge on the target. Impact flash 1–2f. Hold the contact drawing 2f.
5. Follow-through: the blade continues past the target along the same arc, body rotated, back heel lifted. 4f.
6. Settle: the blade stops low or out to the side, the body still. The cut lands in the stillness. Hold 18f+.

Arc: a cut is a circle around the shoulder (one hand) or the center of both hands (two hands). Plot the tip's arc and keep every blade drawing on it.

## Thrust, 4 keys

1. Blade pulled back at the hip, point forward. 2. Anticipation: sink the knees. 3. Extension: front knee bent, back leg straight, arm and blade one straight line to the target; a short straight smear. 4. Recover back to guard. A thrust is the only straight-line blade motion.

## Parry and clash

- Blades meet edge to flat, at an angle, never perfectly crossed at 90°. The contact point is a third down from the tip of the parried blade.
- On contact: 2f hold, a spark burst (effects.md), a 6-frame shake if the sheet allows it, then both blades rebound a short distance along their arcs.
- A bind (blades locked, pushing) is a hold with a 2-frame tremble: alternate two drawings 4px apart.

## Sheathe, 4 keys

Blade raised and turned spine-down; tip finds the scabbard mouth (left hand at the mouth); slide in (slow, then the last third fast); click closed with a 2f hold and a tiny hand settle. The tip entering the scabbard is the moment — hold before it.

## Common mistakes

- Rotating the blade around its center instead of the grip.
- The blade passing through the body. Rotate the wrist or draw the blade behind the arm.
- One long eased tween for the cut. A cut is 3–4 drawings with a smear.
- The edge facing the wrong way on the follow-through.
- A blade trail made of glow circles. It is one tapered shape.
