# Cloth and hair — follow-through, wind, drag, settle

Secondary motion. It never leads; it follows by one or two drawings and settles after the body stops.

## Rules

- **Drag**: when the body moves, cloth and hair stay behind first, then catch up. The tip of a cape or a long lock lags most.
- **Follow-through**: when the body stops, cloth and hair keep going in the direction of travel, overshoot, then fall back. Two drawings past the stop, one back, then still.
- **Mass**: hair is drawn as 2–3 masses; each mass moves as one shape. Do not animate strands.
- **Weight**: heavy coats move slowly and in big shapes; scarves, ribbons, and light skirts move fast and fold more.
- **Pivots**: hair pivots at the scalp along the part; a cape at the shoulders; a scarf at the neck; a sleeve at the shoulder seam.

## Wind

A wave travels from the pivot to the tip. Draw 3 drawings of a cloth shape where a fold moves down its length, and cycle them on twos (6 frames a cycle). Hair in wind: the masses tilt in the wind direction and the tips lift; the bangs part.

Wind in the world agrees: rain slants the same way, petals and smoke travel the same way, a flag points the same way. Write the wind direction in the bible.

## Settle after an action (the sakuga follow-up)

1. Contact or stop: hair and cloth still traveling. 2. Overshoot: past the rest position. 3. Return: slightly back. 4. Rest. Keys 2–4 fit inside the hold after the settle; this is the "hair follow-through" the timing skill allows inside a hold.

## In rain

Wet hair and cloth are heavier: fewer, larger masses, less lift, drips from the tips (single `line` strokes on ones).

## Common mistakes

- Cloth and hair moving at the same time as the body.
- A cape that stops dead when the figure stops.
- Hair masses changing count between drawings.
